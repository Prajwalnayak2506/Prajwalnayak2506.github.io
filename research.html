Energy

Optimization for shared energy
2026 · Paper

Data centers in a residential renewable energy community

With G. S. Kalojiya and Prof. Anupama Kowli, Department of Electrical Engineering, IIT Bombay

What happens when a neighbourhood that shares solar power also shares its grid connection with a data center? I built a framework to size storage and share energy across 1,833 households and the IIT Bombay data center, and compared four centralized and decentralized storage configurations.

  • An LP-based dispatch framework in Pyomo, solved with HiGHS, with rule-based peer-to-peer allocation between participants.
  • Storage architecture matters more than energy sharing: centralized storage cut annual grid imports by 63.16% against a grid-only baseline.
  • Energy sharing matters most in decentralized setups, where cost-effective data center batteries displace residential storage.
  • Storage topology and battery costs together decide how dependent the community stays on the grid and how much of its own energy it uses locally.

Presented at the Energy Informatics Academy Conference (EI.A 2026) in Beijing, and published by Springer. Read the paper

Oct – Nov 2025

Non-intrusive load monitoring

With Prof. Anupama Kowli, Department of Electrical Engineering, IIT Bombay

A single smart meter sees a whole home’s consumption at once. Non-intrusive load monitoring tries to work out which appliances are running from that one signal, which is hard when there’s very little labeled data to learn from.

  • Built a physics-aware synthetic smart-meter generator from appliance signatures to simulate 20 households.
  • Engineered rolling and cyclic-time features from active and reactive power to separate resistive loads from inductive ones.
  • Developed a binary-relevance ensemble of random forest, XGBoost, and a 1D-CNN, with per-appliance weights optimized by SLSQP.
  • Added classification-gated regression and median filtering so appliances read exactly zero power when they’re off.
  • Reached an average F1 of 0.87 and MAE of 10 W on synthetic data: strongest on the fridge (0.99) and geyser (0.97), weakest on the kettle and washing machine (around 0.80).

Mechanisms

Game theory and mechanism design
Aug 2026 – present

Strategyproof facility location with interval predictions

With Prof. Swaprava Nath, Department of Computer Science and Engineering, IIT Bombay

Imagine choosing where to build a single facility on a line, based on where people say they are. If people can benefit by lying about their location, the outcome falls apart. Learning-augmented mechanism design asks how ML predictions can improve these decisions without opening new ways to manipulate them.

  • Integrating ML confidence intervals that reflect epistemic uncertainty into learning-augmented 1D facility location.
  • Designing mechanisms that resolve the manipulative discontinuities introduced by mixed double-peaked preferences and physical feasibility constraints.
  • Building a simulation with a deviation checker that computationally verifies strategyproofness across all misreports.
  • Using adversarial search to find worst-case instances and evaluate empirical approximation ratios.

  • Game theory
  • Learning-augmented algorithms

Speech

Neural audio coding
Mar 2026 – present

Ultra-low-bitrate neural audio codec

Master’s thesis with Prof. Srikanth Chetupalli, Department of Electrical Engineering, IIT Bombay

At 1.2 kbps, a speech codec has to throw away almost everything and keep only what a listener needs. Generative models can rebuild the rest, which turns compression into a question of what a model can reliably imagine back.

  • Implemented LSPnet, a 1.2 kbps neural speech codec for high-fidelity, low-complexity coding.
  • Developed a continuous flow matching (CFM) decoder to improve quality in neural generative audio models.
  • Implementing stochastic interpolants with latent-space representations for efficient probability-flow sampling, compressed-state modeling, and very-low-latency inference.
  • Benchmarking reconstruction fidelity against baselines with objective perceptual metrics (PESQ, STOI).
  • PyTorch
  • Flow matching
  • Stochastic interpolants
  • PESQ / STOI

Tokenizers

Fairness across languages
Oct 2026 – present

Fair tokenizers for Indian languages

Course project for Optimization in Machine Learning (CS 769), IIT Bombay

Language models don’t read text; they read tokens. If a tokenizer splits Hindi into three times as many pieces as English, Hindi speakers pay more for the same sentence and fit less of it into a model’s context. I’m building Byte Pair Encoding from scratch to study this as an optimization problem.

  • Formulated BPE as a combinatorial problem: minimize the corpus token count with a fixed number of merges.
  • Proved that each merge saves exactly its pair frequency, that greedy savings never increase (so the compression curve is convex), and that encoding is lossless.
  • Built a small corpus where greedy BPE is provably suboptimal, and confirmed the optimum with an exhaustive search.
  • Moved to byte-level BPE and derived exactly how UTF-8 encodes Devanagari and Kannada, predicting that the first Hindi merge is a fragment that isn’t a character at all. The experiment confirmed it: none of the Hindi tokens in the test words was a readable character.
  • Defined a character-boundary violation count as a first measure of linguistic integrity, and measured parity: with merges learned only from English, the Hindi greeting नमस्ते costs 4.5 times as many tokens as “hello”.
  • Next: train on the FLORES-200 parallel corpus in English, Hindi, and Kannada, and treat compression, cross-lingual parity, and akshara integrity as separate objectives.
